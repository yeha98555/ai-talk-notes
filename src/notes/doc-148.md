---
title: AI Tools for Forward Deployed Engineering
speaker: Vasuman Moza, Varick Agents
video: https://youtu.be/l0FLhNqBOic
---
The talk's premise is that AI has already solved the execution of knowledge work — models are intelligent enough and harnesses (browser use, robust MCPs) are reliable enough that end-to-end tasks get done. The bottleneck that remains is how deeply an organization can understand and re-engineer a specific business without scaling headcount exponentially, since every department — a healthcare sales team versus a SaaS sales team, AP versus FP&A — runs differently, and that context is hard to extract from employees and even harder to feed into a model call that doesn't break.

This is the job of the forward deployed engineer (FDE), which the speaker breaks into three parts. First, mapping how humans actually do the work today: embedding with process leads (AP, AR, reconciliation, billing) to learn not just the documented golden path but what really happens when things go wrong — the reality that "Sarah handles it, but escalates to Chris, who takes four days to reconcile a PO against an invoice." Second, re-engineering the process around AI rather than bolting AI onto a broken process (cited as the reason behind high-profile stats on failed GenAI pilots) — deciding, step by step, what becomes fully autonomous, what stays human-in-the-loop, and what stays fully human, while keeping the new workflow recognizable enough that non-technical operators can adopt it. Third, deploying agents on top of existing systems of record — NetSuite, SAP, Dynamics, Salesforce — instead of asking enterprises to migrate, since large companies have sunk years and millions into those systems and won't rip them out for a point solution.

Because it's rare to find people who are both top-tier technically and highly skilled at the human, EQ-heavy work of extracting context from clients, Varick built an internal "FD agent" to let each forward-deployed strategist manage more client relationships without linear headcount growth. The business thesis is that department-wide transformation (25–75% ROI) beats narrow point solutions (5–10% ROI), and that captures the real value of going deep versus wide.

Head of engineering JD Pruitt then walked through the tooling in three stages. The engagement agent is a purpose-built alternative to generic Claude usage: it ingests meeting notes, synthesizes uploaded documentation, reads slides, and resolves entity ambiguity (is "Sarah" from this email the same person as "Sarah" in that Slack message) — the exact grind that was burning out FDEs. The workflow agent embeds that same assistant directly into the platform where FDEs build out client workflows, flagging missed edge cases and unrouted approvals in real time. The still-in-development third stage is a fully autonomous assistant that would read incoming client emails requesting small changes (e.g., rerouting a QC report), reason over the existing process model, and ship the workflow update itself, freeing the FDE for higher-value interviewing and process discovery.

Underneath all three sits a dependency graph as the single source of truth for how a company's processes actually function — enterprise workflows are largely linear but full of approval cycles, and process owners want strict dependency ordering. Two technical problems had to be solved on top of that graph. The first is generating good analysis from extracted context: frontier models like Claude turned out too verbose and lacked the judgment — which the speaker compares to what makes human consultants valuable — of knowing which details actually matter to a client, so Varick post-trains open-source base models (they favor Kimi K2) to hit that balance of detail and clarity. The second is reliably traversing the knowledge graph to pull the right context in the first place; they built a custom RL environment with tools for tasks like disambiguating identical names across documents and detecting redundancy or DAG violations in the graph, training the model to be a good graph-traversal agent rather than relying on an off-the-shelf model's general reasoning.

The closing framing is that knowledge-work execution is close to solved, so the frontier has shifted to designing how work gets done around AI — which is inherently a forward-deployed, audit-first motion (learn the company from the inside, then implement) rather than a pure product play, and is why Varick is betting heavily on building internal AI tooling for its own FDEs.
