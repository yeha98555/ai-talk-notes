---
title: Quantization: The Size vs Quality Trade-Off
speaker: Hugging Face
video: https://youtu.be/-s4KGeAE6iQ
---
This short talk explains quantization as the practical mechanism behind smaller, faster AI models, and the compromise it always entails.

Models are fundamentally large collections of numbers — weights, activations, embeddings. In full precision, each number is stored using 32 or 16 bits. Quantization reduces that bit width: Q8 stores each value in eight bits, Q4 in four bits. Relative to FP32, Q8 shrinks file size roughly fourfold, and Q4 roughly eightfold, which translates directly into smaller downloads, lower memory use, and often faster inference. This compression can be pushed further still — Transformers.js recently added support for Bonsai, a 1.7-billion-parameter language model from Prem AI that uses one-bit weights, bringing its deployed size down to around 290 megabytes.

The trade-off is that fewer bits generally mean less numerical precision, and less precision can degrade model quality. Quantization is therefore not a free lunch but a deliberate choice, one that Transformers.js exposes directly through its DType setting, letting developers pick their own point on the size-versus-quality curve.

The talk also introduces quantization-aware training (QAT), typically a post-training step in which the model learns to operate well under lower-precision constraints before being exported, rather than just having its weights compressed afterward. This aims to preserve more quality through the compression process. Google's Gemma 4 QAT models illustrate this: alongside the regular Gemma 4 instruction model, there are QAT-trained mobile variants designed to retain more of the original quality while sharply cutting memory requirements.

The closing point is that quantization doesn't make a model better — it makes it fit. A slightly degraded but appropriately sized model that actually runs on your hardware is often far more useful in practice than a full-precision model that doesn't.
