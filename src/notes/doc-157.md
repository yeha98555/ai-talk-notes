---
title: Data Quality Is the Compute Multiplier
speaker: Ari Morcos, DatologyAI
video: https://youtu.be/_PdK6x7PQNM
---
The talk opens with a compute-scarcity thesis: H100 prices have risen roughly 40% off their lows, reasoning models burn roughly eight times the tokens of non-reasoning models with a further 5x increase projected, and frontier labs are starting to ration inference (Google capping Meta's Gemini usage, OpenAI selling "token futures"). In a world where compute access itself may become constrained, the argument is that data quality is the lever that turns a fixed compute budget into far more model capability — it makes the learning curve steeper, so the same data/compute/time/dollar budget yields dramatically better performance, or the same performance is reachable with a much smaller budget.

The underlying goal is maximizing marginal information gain per token shown to the model. This requires task-relevant data (there is no universal best dataset — legal use cases need legal data, not healthcare data), diversity (lack of diversity is the source of brittleness, where a model answers correctly only when a question is phrased just so), and correct mixing across many heterogeneous sources.

DatologyAI positions itself as an "oil refinery for data": it doesn't source new tokens but upgrades existing public, proprietary, and licensed tokens through four stages — clean (heuristic filters, benchmark decontamination against downstream evals with low n-gram thresholds to fight benchmaxing), curate (quality classifiers, topic taxonomy and balancing, redundancy reduction, up/down-sampling by quality and relevance, task-distribution matching), create (synthetic rephrasing to expand size and diversity without model collapse, since content still originates from real documents), and compose (sequencing and mixing datasets across the now-standard multi-phase training curricula).

Results are presented across several domains. For vision-language models, curating the ~25B-token Mammoth dataset (used to train the fusion adapter) pushed performance well past the Pareto frontier defined by public VLMs like Qwen3 and InternVL — a 14-point absolute improvement from curation alone, and near-parity with Qwen3.5-4B using ~145x less training compute, with curated models also producing far more concise responses (efficiency gains hold when plotted per-inference-flop too). For multilingual text models, using only 8% multilingual tokens (max ~6B tokens per language), curation again beat the Qwen/Liquid/Cohere frontier with about 8x less compute; curating English data was shown to improve non-English performance and vice versa via cross-lingual transfer, with transfer magnitude correlating with linguistic similarity to English. A trillion-token curated dense Llama-style model's scaling line was shown to predict the performance of a customer's 17-trillion-token sparse model (RC's Trendy Large) trained with roughly 50x more compute, illustrating that correctly curated, token-scarcity-simulated data enables predictable extrapolation to much larger runs, de-risking hero runs before they're launched.

On synthetic data, the "rephrasing" approach transforms real documents into hundreds of templated formats (e.g., true/false questions from a corporate-takeover document). Because content is grounded in source documents, model collapse is avoided, and the rephrasing model only needs to perform an easier transformation task rather than teach the underlying concepts — but which documents get rephrased matters enormously; rephrasing high-quality documents rather than random ones drives the gains.

Two customer case studies illustrate synergy across pipeline stages. With Thomson Reuters, mid-training an open model on 100B curated tokens (a mix of proprietary legal data and public data resembling the pre-training distribution, at under 1% of pre-training compute) lifted legal-bench performance about five points with no catastrophic forgetting of general capability — and crucially, applying Thomson Reuters' existing post-training harness to this mid-trained model nearly tripled the gains from post-training compared to applying it to the default instruction-tuned model, because the starting policy was already more accurate. With RC's Trendy Large, a fully open, US-made, 17-trillion-token model trained entirely on curated public data (no proprietary data, no closed-model distillation) reached performance competitive with GLM-5 and Kimi and beat Claude on some tasks — and the RC team, who had never trained a model before mid-last-year, did this along with several other models for under $20 million total, challenging the assumption that customizing frontier-competitive models requires hundreds of millions of dollars.

The closing framing: prioritize signal per token over raw token count, repeating high-quality data usually beats adding low-quality data, and treat data curation as a frontier research-engineering problem — scoring data along many axes and scaling that scoring to petabytes — because data quality remains the most underleveraged compute multiplier available today.
