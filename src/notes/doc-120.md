---
title: Catching Hallucinations With a Clean-Context Second Opinion
speaker: Ankur Goyal, Braintrust
video: https://youtu.be/ZOVXen0paHk
---
The starting observation is that LLMs hallucinate more, or draw the wrong conclusion, precisely when their context window is stuffed with information. Attention is a scarce resource: as more of the context gets consumed by historical actions, tool outputs, and accumulated state, the model has to search for a needle in a haystack to answer a question — and it can end up locking onto the wrong needle, producing a confidently wrong answer even though the right evidence was technically present somewhere in the transcript.

The fix is to stop asking the same, context-loaded model to both make and check its own claims. Instead, each claim the model produces is paired with the specific raw data that supposedly backs it up — whatever slice of evidence the model itself points to as its source. That claim-plus-evidence pair is then handed to a second model call that starts from a clean context: no history, no clutter, just the claim and the data behind it. That second model is asked a single, narrow question — is this claim actually supported by this data? — with nothing else competing for its attention.

Because that verification pass has no noise to sift through, it functions as a much sharper judge of grounding than trying to catch errors inline, in the same bloated context that produced them in the first place. Separating claim generation from claim verification, and giving the verifier a clean slate, is what reduces the rate of hallucinations slipping through undetected — turning hallucination-catching into a deliberate, structural second opinion rather than something the original, context-saturated model is asked to police in itself.
