---
title: The Missing Layer: Why Semantics and Knowledge Graphs Are Essential for AI-Ready Data Systems
speaker: Juan Sequeda, data.world
video: https://youtu.be/-udYsiECe3o
---
The talk opens by insisting that knowledge graphs are not a 2023 invention: the underlying ideas trace back to 1950s computer science, and text-to-SQL itself has been studied for over 30 years. When ChatGPT launched in late 2022, everyone rediscovered "chat with your data" and produced a wave of cute text-to-SQL demos, but these stayed toy-level because they ignored semantics. Pressed by Snowflake in summer 2023 to define what "semantics" actually means, the speaker's team ran a benchmark comparing plain text-to-SQL against a setup where the LLM was given an OWL ontology and asked to write SPARQL (translated under the hood into SQL via a virtualized knowledge graph). The knowledge-graph approach was three times more accurate — later refined to roughly 4x — a result widely cited afterward (including in Neo4j's GraphRAG manifesto) and echoed by Gartner naming semantic data integration and knowledge graphs a top data-engineering trend.

The core problem now is that "semantic layer" has become an overused marketing term, so the talk lays out a maturity spectrum: a business glossary (agreed-upon terms across teams) comes first, then a controlled vocabulary (fixed definitions, e.g., for reference data and statuses), then the BI-flavored semantic layer people mean today (dimensions, measures, metrics), then taxonomies (hierarchical broader/narrower relationships), then ontologies (concepts, relationships, and axioms like cardinality constraints), and finally knowledge graphs — nodes and edges that can uniformly represent relational tables, tree-shaped JSON/XML, and NLP-extracted entities. Google coined and popularized the term in 2012, but the machinery — RDF (1998), OWL, SKOS, SHACL, PROV, DCAT — comes from decades of W3C semantic-web standards that LLMs, trained on the web, already understand well.
